Sir Timothy John Berners-Lee

Inventor of the World Wide Web

Biography

Tim Berners-Lee was born on 8 June 1955 in London, England. His parents, Conway Berners-Lee and Mary Lee Woods, were mathematicians who worked on the Ferranti Mark 1, one of the first commercially sold computers.

He studied physics at The Queen's College, Oxford, and graduated in 1976. After working as a software engineer in the UK, he took a contract at CERN in Switzerland in 1980, where he built ENQUIRE, an early program for linking related pieces of information.

In March 1989, while back at CERN, he wrote a proposal for a system that would let scientists share documents across computers using links. By the end of 1990 he had written the first web browser, the first web server, and the first website.

In 1994 he founded the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) at MIT to guide the Web's development through open standards. He was knighted by Queen Elizabeth II in 2004 and received the A.M. Turing Award, often called the "Nobel Prize of computing," in 2016.

Impact on the World

He invented the Web

He created the three core technologies the Web still runs on today:

  • HTML – the language used to write web pages
  • HTTP – the rules for sending pages between computers
  • URL – the address system for finding any page

He gave it away for free

Berners-Lee chose not to patent his invention. On 30 April 1993, CERN released the Web's software into the public domain so anyone could use and build on it without paying royalties. That decision is a major reason the Web spread so quickly.

He keeps the Web open

Through the W3C, the World Wide Web Foundation (co-founded in 2009), and the Solid project, he has spent decades advocating for:

  • Open, shared standards that work on every device and browser
  • Net neutrality and universal access to the Web
  • Personal privacy and people's control over their own data

"This is for everyone"

At the opening ceremony of the 2012 London Olympics, he appeared at a computer and sent a message that summed up his vision:

This is for everyone.
